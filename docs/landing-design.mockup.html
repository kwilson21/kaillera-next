▶Intro · 0:50

Why it feels close to the couch

Rollback netplay runs the game at full speed on your machine and predicts what your opponent pressed. When a prediction misses, it rewinds a few frames and replays, quietly. The network lag is still there; rollback hides it from you. Under about 150 ms of round trip, it feels like playing offline. Lockstep, the old way, waits the full round trip on every frame.

Don't take our word for it.

Rollback netplay runs your game locally without waiting on the network. Every player's input, yours included, takes effect after a small fixed delay (a frame or two, just enough to absorb jitter), so both machines apply it on the same frame. For the rest of the round-trip, the engine predicts what the opponent pressed and keeps going, then rewinds and replays if the guess was wrong.

The proof is the contrast: at the same simulated RTT, rollback keeps your input delay small while lockstep makes every press arrive noticeably later. The rollback button switches between them, and auto-compare switches every 6 seconds.
