kaillera-next

Privacy

Last updated October 29, 2026

kaillera-next uses session diagnostics to investigate connection, emulator, and synchronization problems. Gameplay images are enabled by default and can be turned off. This notice explains the app's behavior and the limits of its image controls.

Your gameplay-image choice

On the play page, turn off Share gameplay images for diagnostics to stop new diagnostic canvas captures and image uploads from this browser. This includes periodic screenshots, images attached to freeze reports, and desync-vision captures. The choice is saved in this browser when browser storage is available. Opening the game with screenshots=off or knperf=light in its URL also forces these captures off.

Images show the gameplay canvas, not your desktop. Turning images off does not turn off non-image session diagnostics, change another participant's settings, or delete images already sent. It also does not stop the gameplay video streams used for streaming play and spectators.

Public-room listing is a separate host choice: its checkbox explicitly allows the live gameplay preview on the public room board and invite cards. Password-protected rooms cannot be publicly listed. Turning off diagnostic images also stops new preview-frame uploads from this browser, but does not unlist the room or immediately remove an existing preview. Enabling diagnostic images alone does not list a room.

What the service receives

Session diagnostics can include your nickname, room and match identifiers, player slot, browser user-agent information, timing and connection statistics, gameplay-input audit records, synchronization hashes, errors, and diagnostic events. The server stores salted IP-address hashes with certain records for correlation and abuse prevention. A hash is not a guarantee of anonymity: records can still be associated with a nickname or session. Servers necessarily receive network addresses when handling requests.

When gameplay images are enabled, the service can receive periodic gameplay screenshots and images for freeze or desync investigation. Diagnostic records and images are available to service administrators; the separately enabled public-room preview is public.

If you submit feedback, the service receives your category and message, an optional email address, the page you are on, and automatically attached context. That context can include the page URL, browser user-agent, timestamp, nickname, room or match, player slot, peer connection states, and session statistics. Feedback also has a salted IP-address hash. Avoid including secrets or other information you do not want the service operator to receive.

Peer connections and providers

Multiplayer uses WebRTC. Other participants may learn your network address as part of establishing a direct connection. The browser contacts STUN services to discover connectivity; the default list includes Cloudflare and Google. If configured and needed, TURN services relay the connection. Those providers receive network-connection information, including IP addresses. The operator may configure different STUN or TURN services. Disabling gameplay images does not disable these connections.

The app includes optional Anthropic Claude image analysis for desync investigations. The server sends selected gameplay images with diagnostic prompts and player-slot labels to Anthropic only when the operator has set both DESYNC_VISION_ENABLED=1 and an ANTHROPIC_API_KEY. Its verdict can be stored with the match diagnostics. This describes a conditional feature, not a claim that it is enabled on the current deployment. Turning gameplay images off prevents new images from this browser being submitted for that feature; it does not recall previously uploaded images.

The service uses its configured database and image/blob storage, which support local storage or Cloudflare D1 and R2. Hosting, network, storage, and any enabled AI providers process data needed to provide their services. Their own retention practices are not set by the game's image checkbox.

Storage and retention

The app's automatic match-cleanup defaults are 7 days after an ordinary match ends (or starts, if no end was recorded), and 7 days after a flagged match is resolved. Unresolved flagged matches default to cleanup after 180 days without being touched. Crashes, freezes, desyncs, and feedback can flag a match for investigation. These windows are configurable by the operator; they describe cleanup eligibility, not a promise of immediate deletion.

Match cleanup covers associated session logs, stored screenshots, desync records, metrics, and linked client events. Feedback records are not removed by that match-cleanup routine. A retention period for feedback or hosting/provider logs has not been established in this notice, and the match windows should not be read as applying to those records or to provider-held copies.

The browser also stores preferences and may cache ROMs locally for your convenience. Clearing site data removes local preferences and caches, not records already sent to the service.
